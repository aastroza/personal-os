# Methodology — why this system is shaped the way it is

The engine isn't just a scheduler. It encodes a philosophy of *designing* a week rather than
reacting to it, and of getting better at it over time. The core beliefs, and their sources:

## Design the week, don't react to it
Set the week's shape from your goals and energy before the calendar fills with other people's
requests. One imperdible outcome per week; deep-work protected in the mornings; comms and admin
boxed into bounded blocks. Inspired by **Robin Sharma's Weekly Design System** (intentional
weekly architecture, protected "creativity" time) and the "same time, same place" discipline of
a recurring planning ritual.

## Energy is the throttle
Declared energy (x/5) caps total load before any goal does. Low energy means a lighter week with
slack — not a compressed backlog. Sustainable intensity: accelerate within your non-negotiables,
not above them; sprints followed by recovery. Aligned with **Tal Raviv's "way of work"** idea of
building a system around how you actually operate, not an idealized version.

## Non-negotiables first
Faith/reflection, family, rest, exercise, quality time are protected before any work is placed.
A week that sacrifices these to look productive is a failed week. Morning routine as the anchor
of the day (landing → deep work → only then screening inbox/messages) draws on the **"win the
morning" tradition** (e.g. structured morning routines; identity-and-intention framing in the
spirit of **Joe Dispenza's** "mentally rehearse the day before you live it").

## Learn from the gap (compounding)
The system's edge is the loop: compare what you planned against what actually happened, capture
the lesson in a persistent log, and let the next plan read it. Over weeks the estimates and the
shape of the week get more accurate. Fixes are structural — a failed case corrects the rules
(behavior-spec), not just this week's plan.

## No vibes, just evals
Every draft is graded pass/fail against an explicit test set before you see it, with red flags
surfaced first. The evals are the exam; the behavior-spec is the student; the plan is the answer
sheet. This keeps the assistant honest and makes quality measurable rather than felt.
